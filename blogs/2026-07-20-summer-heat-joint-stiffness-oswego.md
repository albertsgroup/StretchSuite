# Why Summer Heat Makes Your Joints Feel Stiffer

With Oswego Harborfest filling the lakefront this week and temperatures climbing across Central New York, clients keep asking the same question: why do I feel stiffer in summer, not looser? It seems backwards, but there is a simple explanation and a few easy fixes.

## Why does hot weather make your joints feel stiffer?

Heat makes you sweat more, and that fluid loss pulls directly from the water your joints need to stay cushioned. Once your body is even mildly dehydrated, the synovial fluid that lubricates your hips, knees, and shoulders gets thicker and less effective, so everyday movement feels tighter.

This is why a lot of people in Oswego, NY notice more stiffness in July and August than in a mild spring month, even though it is warmer out. Humidity adds to the effect, since your body works harder to cool itself and loses fluid faster than you realize.

## Does dehydration really affect how my joints move?

Yes. Research on joint health shows that even mild dehydration can measurably increase stiffness and soreness within hours, especially after standing or walking for long stretches.

That matters in Central New York this time of year, when outdoor festivals, yard work, and long walks downtown are part of daily life. Drinking water steadily throughout the day, not just when you feel thirsty, keeps your joints moving the way they should.

## How can I stay loose at Harborfest and other Oswego summer events?

Short movement breaks every hour, paired with steady hydration, keep your hips, knees, and lower back from locking up during a long day on your feet. A few minutes of gentle stretching between sets or vendor stops makes a real difference by the time the fireworks start.

Try rolling your ankles, shifting your weight side to side instead of locking your knees, and reaching overhead every hour.
