## Why does cold weather make your joints feel stiff?

Cold air thickens the fluid that lubricates your joints and tightens the muscles around them, which naturally shrinks your range of motion. Your body also pulls blood flow toward your core to stay warm, leaving less circulation reaching your hands, knees, and feet.

A few things work against you at once in winter.

- Synovial fluid, the natural lubricant in your joints, gets thicker and less slippery in the cold.
- Blood vessels in your arms and legs narrow, reducing circulation to the joints that need it most.
- Shorter days and icy sidewalks mean less daily movement, and less movement means more stiffness.

None of this means something is wrong with you. It is simply how bodies respond to cold.

## Does a Central New York winter make stiffness worse than elsewhere?

Yes, long, cold winters in Central New York intensify joint stiffness because people spend more consecutive months indoors and moving less. Oswego's lake-effect snow season can stretch for weeks on end, and that adds up fast.

That is why so many of our Oswego, NY clients notice their stiffest months run December through March. Consistent movement, even in small doses, counteracts most of it.

## How can you stay flexible during a Central New York winter?

Keep moving indoors every day, even briefly, and stretch before you head out into the cold. Warming up your hips, hamstrings, and shoulders for a few minutes makes a real difference before shoveling or walking the dog.

Simple habits that help most Oswego County clients:

- Stretch for five to ten minutes each morning before your joints have to work.
- Layer up, especially around your knees and hands, before heading outside.
- Take short movement breaks indoors instead of sitting for hours at a time.
- Book regular assisted stretching sessions to maintain range of motion all season.

Yes, better hip, ankle, and core flexibility improves balance, which lowers your risk of slipping on ice or snow. Stiff joints react slower, and that lag is often the difference between a stumble and a fall.
