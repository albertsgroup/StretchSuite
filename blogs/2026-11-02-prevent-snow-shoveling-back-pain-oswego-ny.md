## Why Does Shoveling Snow Hurt Your Back?

Shoveling hurts your back because it combines heavy lifting, twisting, and cold, tight muscles all at once. National emergency room data shows lower back injuries make up more than a third of all shoveling related visits, mostly from overexertion rather than falls.

Cold air makes muscles stiffer, so a motion that feels easy in July can strain a tight hamstring or lower back in January. Add a heavy, wet shovelful of snow and a twisting toss, and you have a recipe for a pulled muscle.

## How Should You Warm Up Before Shoveling Snow?

Spend five to ten minutes warming up your legs, hips, and back before you pick up a shovel. A short walk around the house, followed by a few gentle stretches, wakes up the muscles you are about to rely on.

Focus on the hamstrings, hips, and lower back, since these do most of the work when you bend and lift. A few arm circles and shoulder rolls help too, since pushing and lifting snow strains the shoulders as well.

## What Stretches Help Prevent Shoveling Injuries?

A few targeted stretches, done consistently, go a long way toward keeping your back safe through a Central New York winter. Try a standing hamstring stretch, a gentle hip flexor stretch, and slow cat and cow movements for the spine.

## Is Snow Shoveling Riskier for Adults Over 50?

Yes, adults over 50 face a higher risk from shoveling, mainly from slipping on ice and from the strain sudden heavy exertion puts on the heart. If you have any history of heart or back issues, check with your doctor before tackling a big snowfall.

Wear boots with real traction, push snow instead of lifting when you can, and take breaks every few minutes. Lifting smaller loads and walking the snow to its new spot, rather than tossing it, protects your back far more than most people realize.
